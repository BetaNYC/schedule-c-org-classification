Schedule C Funding of Arts and Cultural Organizations

New York City Council discretionary funding to non-profits and agency initiatives (colloquially known as Schedule C funding) is a large source of funding for cultural and arts organizations in New York City. Getting a complete view of Schedule C funding of the sector (or any single sector, by extension) is not straightforward as:

  • expense and capital funding disclosure data are released by fiscal year, via Excel files on the Council’s website, and
  • awards are not classified or categorized beyond their Source, the initiative or provision from which the funds are awarded.

There are two immediate ways to identify awards to arts and cultural organizations:

  1. The initiative or provision in Source explicitly references arts or culture
  2. The contracting city agency is the Department of Cultural Affairs (Agency: DCLA)

There are 173 funding initiatives for FY 2027. Those that explicitly apply to arts and cultural organizations are:

  • Coalition Theaters of Color
  • Cultural After-School Adventure (CASA)
  • Cultural Immigrant Initiative
  • Film Accelerator Model
  • SU-CASA
  • Support for Arts Instruction
  • Community Composting at Cultural Institutions Groups
  • Creative Arts Team

Filtering for the above arts and culture funding initiatives returns 1,257 of 10,040 awards, a total of 12.5%. These awards comprise only 5.6% of total Schedule C funds, $39,810,000 out of $705,564,000

This approach is limited by the omission of organizations whose Schedule C award(s) funding source do not easily indicate their engagement in arts or cultural work.

Another pathway to Schedule C award classification is to use Federal IRS data. The IRS classifies nonprofit organizations using the National Taxonomy of Exempt Entities (NTEE) system, which was developed by the National Center For Charitable Statistics in the 1980s. Nonprofits are assigned an NTEE code during the tax-exempt application process. The NCCS outlines appropriate uses of NTEE codes as follows:

The National Taxonomy of Exempt Entities (NTEE) is a descriptive taxonomy that helps regulators and researchers aggregate data for statistical analysis. While certain nonprofit characteristics like the assigned 501(c) subsection code can have significant binding implications such as whether a donation can be treated as tax-deductible (501c3 public charities) or NOT (most other 501c types). The NTEE classification, on the other hand, should not be used as a limiting factor by funders or other institutional actors. The NTEE is often not precise and many nonprofits have missions that span multiple NTEE categories. It is NOT recommended to ever eliminate nonprofits from funding considerations because of their NTEE code or assume that their NTEE category captures their full range of activities.

Nonprofit NTEE codes are included in the IRS’ Business Master File (BMF) data. BMF data may be joined to the Schedule C disclosure records via Federal Tax ID numbers (EINs).
